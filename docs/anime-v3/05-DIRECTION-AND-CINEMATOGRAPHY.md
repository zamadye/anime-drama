# Direction & cinematography
Status: production specification.

## Shot record
ID, scene, duration, framing, subject, dramatic purpose, stage blocking, actor view/clip, eyeline, camera keys, lighting, effects, dialogue IDs, sound cues and cut. Shot duration is integer frames; local shot time maps to global episode time.

Supported target grammar: establishing/wide, two-shot, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, over-shoulder, reaction and insert. Perspective elevations need corresponding drawn actor/background views. A 2D zoom is not a new camera angle.

## Continuity
Draw axis of action and screen positions before coverage. Maintain eyeline and 180-degree rule unless deliberately motivated and documented. Match hand/prop position across cuts. Track head and body orientation independently. A reaction shot must have a changed performance, not only a different crop.

## Camera
Static, pan, tracking, push/pull and rack focus. Start with fixed-camera acting tests. In a production shot, every move has an emotional/spatial reason. No always-on handheld drift. Use easing appropriate to weight; avoid floating mechanical pans.

## Effects
Rain, wind, light shafts, rim light, tears, depth blur and foreground particles are authored per beat. Avoid stacking effects to mask weak character art. Set numeric intensity/count limits and record benchmark. Use precomputed/static background blur when needed; don't blur face to hide cutout artifacts.

## Staging
Separate background/midground/foreground; give floor anchors, horizon, scale and occluders. Establishing and close-up must describe the same geography. Face highlights respond to light direction. Production palette remains B; do not silently switch to painterly A.

## Storyboard review packet
Thumbnail per shot + dialogue + action + seconds + transition + required asset list. Include missing assets explicitly. Produce an animatic before polish. Review is invalidated by substantial changes to dialogue, action or shot order.
